How I write

Engineers can smell marketing from a mile away.

So I write the way good engineers talk: specific, a little dry, and useful before it's clever. Three rules I work by.

Rule one

Say the true thing first.

The reader already knows prep is hard. Copy that admits it earns the next sentence. Copy that pretends otherwise gets scrolled past.

Rule two

Every line has a test case.

Before a line ships, I ask what it should make the reader do, and how we'd know. If there's no answer, the line gets cut.

Rule three

Stay until it's done.

A campaign isn't finished when the copy is approved. It's finished when the numbers come back and the next draft is better for it.

07 · Brand storytellingLong form

At 11:40 pm, Ananya submitted her 212th solution. It failed.

She'd been working a service-desk job for two years, writing code at night on a laptop with a sticky "E" key. Nobody at work knew. Her parents thought she was watching shows.

At 11:52 she found the bug. An off-by-one error, the kind she'd have missed six months ago.

Four months later, she explained that same bug to an interviewer.

Every offer letter starts at night.

Why it worksBrand storytelling for a learning platform shouldn't star the platform. It should star the learner. The product appears only as the place where the small, private progress happened.

The sticky "E" key and the parents who think she's watching shows do the heavy lifting. Details like these make a reader think "that's me" before they think "that's an ad."

The closing line is built to travel: it works as a campaign tagline, a video end card, or a caption.
